One of the underrated joys of a Madrid bike tour is what comes after: guilt-free eating. After two or three hours in the saddle, you've more than earned a proper Madrid feed. Here's where our guides head when the bikes are parked.

## Breakfast: Churros at San Ginés

If you're on an early morning tour, there is no better finish line than **Chocolatería San Ginés** (Pasadizo de San Ginés, 5). Open since 1894, this Madrid institution serves crispy churros with thick hot chocolate 24 hours a day. Order a porras (the fat churros) and dunk enthusiastically.

**Price:** Around €5 per person.

## Mid-Morning: Bocadillo de Calamares

Madrid's most beloved street food is the *bocadillo de calamares* — a crusty baguette stuffed with fried squid rings. The best are found in the bars around Plaza Mayor and La Latina. **Bar La Campana** (Calle Botoneras, 6) is our local favourite.

**Price:** €2.50–3.50.

## Lunch: Mercado de San Miguel

The **Mercado de San Miguel** (Plaza de San Miguel) is a beautiful iron-and-glass market from 1916, now filled with some of Madrid's best food stalls. Graze your way through jamón ibérico, fresh oysters, pintxos, and local wines. It gets busy after 13:00 — arrive early or expect to wait.

**Price:** €15–25 per person for a proper grazing session.

## Lunch (Budget Option): Menú del Día

Almost every neighbourhood restaurant in Madrid offers a *menú del día* — a three-course set lunch with wine for around €12–15. You get a starter, main, dessert, bread, and a drink. It is the best-value meal in the city and a genuine cultural institution.

Ask your guide to recommend one near the tour end point.

## Afternoon: Vermouth in La Latina

The **La Latina** neighbourhood is the epicentre of Madrid's *vermuteo* (vermouth) culture. Dozens of traditional bars serve house vermouth poured from the tap, alongside anchovies, olives, and chips. The ritual is simple: order, nibble, chat, repeat.

Our favourite bar: **El Tempranillo** (Calle de la Cava Baja, 38).
